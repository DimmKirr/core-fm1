# The predictive CPU guard (`CPU_GUARD`)

Builder item `CPU_GUARD` (System; off by default), flag `FELUCCA_CPU_GUARD`. After Flowstate's guard by Zakaria
Chowdhury (`zakariachowdhury/flowstate-fm1` `e62e186`, `firmware/src/guard.c`, `docs/guardrails.md` §2.5,
GPL-3.0-only): only its CPU part, reworked for Optimist. Code: `firmware/src/system/cpuguard.c`, `cpuguard.h`; the model:
`firmware/src/system/cpuguard_costs.h`, generated by `tools/builder/cpu_costs.py`; tests: `tests/cpuguard_test.c`.

All numbers come from the emulator (it counts instructions, not cycles) and host builds. Nothing has run on an FM-1.

## What it does

Without the guard, `audio.c` sheds one voice before the next half after any half that took more than 85 % of its
time. A single heavy half sheds a note. The shed can be the bass: three ACID lines lost 80 notes in 5 s in
scenario 7 below.

With the guard, `audio.c` calls `cg_pre()` before each DMA half and `cg_post(us)` after it.

- **The load.** It is the larger of two values:
  - the half's measured time;
  - a prediction: the last half's measured load, plus what the cost model says changed since (voices started or
    ended).

  The prediction starts from a measurement, so what the guard eased shows in it at once. The model only adds the
  change.
- **Step up.** The guard goes up one level when:
  - the load stays over 85 % for 8 halves in a row (46 ms); or
  - a half runs late (100 %) right after one over 85 %; or
  - after a half over 85 % (not itself late), the next half is predicted to run late. The step then happens
    before that half renders.

  A lone late half after a normal one is a spike, not a load (a flash write, another interrupt's burst), and steps
  nothing, as `audio.c`'s overload fade (SLOOP 2.3, always built) already treated it. Until 2026-10-12 it stepped at once, and its own
  time counted as what the level saved (below), so the level never came off: on the user's FM-1, at ~41 % load,
  "once after some time the reverb stops producing any sound" (the REVERB level, held for ever).

  A level with nothing to ease in what sounds now is skipped. An FM6-only mix goes straight to shedding.
- **The levels**, in order:
  1. **Quality**:
     - ACID renders without oversampling (X0X's lite mode of the 303, `bass303_set_lite`);
     - ANALOG 2's swarm plays at most 2 copies;
     - the drum tails end sooner: a synthesised drum voice under -40 dBFS (not -72), an X0X 808 voice 40 dB under
       its hit's peak (not 60). Nothing is cut that sounds over that; a tail ends without a fade (a step of at most
       1 % of full scale, under a mix that is overloaded).
  2. **UNISON**: a UNISON part plays at most 2 voices. The extra voices fade out at the step, and new notes start
     2.
  3. **Reverb** (since 2026-10-12, `revcrack`): the reverb bus goes off (`fx.c rev_guard`). Its wet fades out
     over one half (8 blocks, as a TYPE change), then the tank is not run and its send is not heard; its line is
     cleared 512 samples a block (from XIP: no 16 or 32 KB clear in one overloaded half), then its filters. When
     the level comes off, the send comes back into a silent tank and a new tail builds up. Why: a running reverb
     costs about as much as the rest of an idle mix (the mots profile, FDN8 in the pool, SIZE 124, emulator at
     96 MHz: 584 -> 1463 us a half for the tail alone), and a tail sounds on with nothing left to shed. Before this
     level, a reverb that did not fit kept every half late: a click every block (the emulator at 24 MHz: 430 of
     432 halves late). Now two late halves in a row step here and the rest of the mix plays on clean (24 MHz: a
     few late halves in 5 s). Its saving is measured as any level's, so it stays off until the reverb would fit
     again. (The user's crackles on the FM-1 that prompted this level were not an overload: measured there, ~41 %
     load, worst half 63 %, no late half; their cause was SIMD=1. The level protects slower builds.)
     The FX returns (2026-10-12, `fx.c fx_ret_block`): REV RET (the mixer's MASTER row, Returns) scales the reverb's
     wet together with this fade (the fade's gain times the return, so at unity the fade is the one above, sample
     for sample). REV RET at 0 turns the bus off the same way at once (no fade: its gain is 0), in every build with
     the reverb, the guard built or not; the tank is then not run, so the guard has nothing to ease there
     (`rev_live` 0). CHO RET and DLY RET at 0 stop their buses too, their lines cleared 512 samples a block.
  4. **Notes**: as before, a half over 85 % sheds one voice before the next. A half predicted late fades out at
     once (2 blocks) as many voices as bring the prediction under 85 %, at most 4. Shedding never touches:
     - a MONO, LEGATO or UNISON part (the bass and the lead);
     - a monophonic engine (ACID);
     - the drums;
     - the only voice still held.
- **Release.** A level comes off after 2 s (345 halves) under 80 % (Flowstate's 1/16 under the ceiling). The guard
  adds what that level saved to the load before it compares: the drop in the measured load over the 8 halves after
  the step, from the load before it (the measured load over the ~8 halves before, each held to a whole half: never
  a spike's own time). So a level comes off only when the music it held back would fit again, and it does not come
  and go every 2 s. Levels with nothing left to ease come off with it, and so does REVERB while the reverb is on
  (coming down from the top it would turn the reverb off, though the reverb was not what had to go).
- **No second step right after one.** The prediction before a half starts from the last half's measurement, which
  does not show what the step just saved yet. So the half right after a step never steps early on a prediction
  (a single late half used to step twice: the reverb off, then voices shed).
- **On screen.** GLO > SYSTEM CPU shows `%G1` .. `%G4` while a level holds (SLOOP's UI). The CDC console's `status`
  prints `cpu_guard`, `cpu_guard_steps`, `cpu_guard_shed`, `cpu_guard_early`, `cpu_guard_avg_pct` (the load over
  ~8 halves), `cpu_guard_step_pct` (the half before the last step), `cpu_guard_rev_gain_pct`, and with the reverb
  `rev_state` (0 on, 1 fading out, 2 clearing, 3 off), `rev_live`, `rev_ret`; also `audio_late`.
- **Under the ceiling the guard does nothing.**
  - The host never steps: it has no clock.
  - `regress` built with the guard renders every golden unchanged.
  - In the emulator at 192 MHz (optimist ac2f1f2), scenarios 2, 7 and 10 render the same audio bit for bit with and without the guard
    (10: at a 2-frame offset from boot timing). Scenario 1 differs only after 3.2 s: without the guard, one half at
    87 % shed a voice; the guard kept it. Neither had a late half.

## The cost model

`cpuguard_costs.h` holds target instructions a sample:
- the idle mix;
- the drum groove;
- one sounding voice per engine UID;
- one sounding X0X channel (the 909's 11 voices, the 808's 13 lanes: `drum_x0x.c`, not `drums.v` voices), and
  their mix once while any of them sounds.

`tools/builder/cpu_costs.py` generates it; nothing in it is hand-tuned.
- **Inputs:**
  - `tests/cpu_baseline.txt`, which `regress` writes (`BUDGET_UPDATE=1`);
  - the `cpu` section of `tools/builder/costs.json`.
- **A voice:** the median over the engine's presets of (preset − idle mix) / its voices (8; VOICE 4, ACID 1).
- **Engines with no baseline entry** (SLICE, PHYS: not built when the baseline was taken) take the largest measured
  voice.
- **Re-runs:** `measure_costs.py` writes the header again after each run and keeps the `cpu` section.
- **Check:** `cpu_costs.py --check` (in `make test`) fails when the header is not what its inputs give.

**Calibration** (`costs.json` `cpu`, 2026-10-06). The host counts Mac instructions and the target counts pi32v2
instructions. In the emulator at 96 MHz, the guard ran on scenarios 1, 2, 3, 7 and 10 with its actions switched off.
It recorded its model's load against the measured load:

| Scenario | Measured / model (at 100 %) |
|---|---|
| 1: FM6 ×3 (8 voices) + drums | 1.236 |
| 2: ANALOG SUPER PAD ×3 | 1.042 |
| 3: FM6, ANALOG, FM6 | 1.153 |
| 7: ACID ×3 + drums | 1.117 |
| 10: FM6 MARK I 8-note cluster + drums | 1.181 |

Each scale is the smallest of these ratios, so the model stays at or under the measured load:
- `scale_pct` 104 (all scenarios);
- FM6 118 (scenarios 1 and 10);
- ACID 111 (scenario 7);
- X0X 122 (scenario 8 on the X0X 909 and 808 kits, x0x-drums: 1.265 and 1.222; added 2026-10-07).

**X0X channels.** `regress` built with the X0X kits keeps each channel sounding (a hit again as it ends:
`cpu/x0x/chNN`), then all of one machine's at once (`cpu/x0x/909_all`, `808_all`). A machine's shared work is
(the sum of its channels - all at once) / (channels - 1): 909 181, 808 203 host instructions a sample. Most of it is
common to both machines (the summed channels' one pass of the mix), so the model counts the smaller once
(`CG_X0X_SHARED`) and each channel net of its own machine's. The 909's crash and ride (samples) cost almost
nothing past that; the 808's cymbal the most (479 target instructions a sample).

**The COMP insert** (FX slots, `fx.c tcomp_run`; DYN since 2026-10-14). The current model is the generated header's (`firmware/src/system/cpuguard_costs.h`, regenerated by `cpu_costs.py`): `CG_COST_TCOMP` 31 (a part's), `CG_COST_DBCOMP` 69 (the drum bus's), `CG_COST_DBDIST` 128, and for DYN's limiter `CG_COST_TLIM` 76. The first measurement (2026-10-08, superseded by the header): `regress` played DIGITAL's first preset (8 notes) on part 1 with COMP in a slot, at 0 (`cpu/fx/comp_off`) and at 127 (`cpu/fx/comp_on`), 1,094 and 1,144 host instructions a sample, so one running insert was 50 host, 52 target. The model adds it for each part that sounds while its insert runs: its amount not 0
with COMP in a slot and the part's FX on, or the insert still letting go of its reduction (it then costs one
compare a block, as off).

**The drum bus's inserts** (FX slots phase 4, `fx.c dbus_run`). Current model: `CG_COST_DBDIST` 128 and `CG_COST_DBCOMP` 69 (cpuguard_costs.h). `regress` plays the drum groove with every drum bus amount at 0 (`cpu/fx/dbus_off`), with the drum track's DST at 127 (`cpu/fx/dbus_dist`) and with its COMP at 127 in S1 (`cpu/fx/dbus_comp`). First measurement, 2026-10-08, superseded by cpuguard_costs.h: 800, 929 and 862 host instructions a sample, so DIST 129 host / 134 target and COMP 62 host / 64 target. DIST runs a state on each channel that carries a signal (L, R and the sends the drum sounds fill: three in the groove), counted while any bus channel carries a signal with DIST on; COMP is one detector and a gain on the five channels, counted while its amount is set or it still lets go. The drum
track's own sends cost a multiply-add a sample each (not modelled).

**The track insert slots** (2026-10-12, fx_slots.c `ins_slot`, fx.c `ins_part` / `dbus_ins`; design D9). Each track
has two insert slots (DIST, FILTER or COMP), so every insert can run on every track: the model counts each slot that
runs, never switches one off. `regress` plays DIGITAL's first preset (8 notes) on part 1 with both its slots at 0
(`cpu/fx/part_off`, 1192 host instructions a sample), its S1 DIST at 127 (`cpu/fx/part_dist`, 1236) and its S2 FILTER
low-pass (`cpu/fx/part_filt`, 1227, a TRK_FILT build: `regress_tf`), and the drum groove with the drum track's FILTER
low-pass (`cpu/fx/dbus_filt`, 1046 against `dbus_off`'s 869): `CG_COST_PDIST` 46, `CG_COST_PFILT` 36, `CG_COST_DBFILT`
184 target. A part's DIST counts while its amount is set (in a slot, FX on), a FILTER while it is not at rest (set, or
gliding open), COMP as before. The worst case, `cpu/fx/ins_worst` (three full parts with S1 DIST 127 and S2 COMP 127,
the drum bus S1 DIST 127 and S2 FILTER, the groove): 2661 host against `cpu/mix/3parts_full_drums`' 2122, so +539 host,
+561 target a sample (26 % of the 2,177 a sample of 96 MHz); the model gives 3 x (46 + 31) + 128 + 184 = 543. At
**QUALITY** the drum bus's DIST and FILTER run on L / R only (the echoes take the bus before them): the model counts
2 / 5 of their cost, and QUALITY is a step worth taking while either runs.

**The further insert types** (2026-10-13, fx/insfx/insfx.c: FOLD FUZZ PHSR FLNG FRMT WIDE, builder items INS_*).
`regress_insfx` (every one built) plays the same part with one of them in S1 at 127 (`cpu/fx/part_<t>`) and the
groove with the drum track's S1 at 127 (`cpu/fx/dbus_<t>`), each less the run's own `part_off` / `dbus_off` (stored
against the baseline's): `CG_COST_PINS` {40, 55, 66, 64, 78, 78} and `CG_COST_DBINS` {72, 69, 132, 127, 131, 74}
(the first six entries of the header's tables, which now run on to CHRS ECHO GATER) target a sample (a part's 1.8 .. 3.6 %, the drum bus's 3.2 .. 6.1 % of the 2,177 of 96 MHz). Each counts while its
amount is set (in a slot, FX on) or while it fades out (2.9 ms). On the drum bus they run on L / R only at every
level, so QUALITY does not scale them.

**CHRS and ECHO** (2026-10-13, fx/insfx/fxi_kern.c, builder counts INS_CHORUS INS_DELAY): measured the same way,
`CG_COST_PINS` 78 / 47 and `CG_COST_DBINS` 73 / 55 (a part's 3.6 / 2.2 %, the drum bus's 3.4 / 2.5 %; on the bus one
instance runs on its mid, out to L and R). A track holding one counts only while it has an instance (fx/insfx/fxi.c):
a track left without one is bypassed and costs nothing, so the guard's worst case grows with the counts built, not
with the slots.

**The fx-rework terms** (2026-10-14 and 2026-10-15, `cpuguard.c`, constants in `cpuguard_costs.h`; target
instructions a sample, as the header holds them):
- **DYN** (the old COMP, now the compressor and a look-ahead limiter in one): `CG_COST_TCOMP` 31 for a part's
  compressor, plus `CG_COST_TLIM` 76 while a track's CEIL limiter runs (the drum track's, L and R, counts twice).
  The master's limiter was already in the idle mix (`CG_COST_BASE`); the master's DYN counts the drum bus's compressor
  term (`CG_COST_DBCOMP`) while its threshold is set.
- **DUST** as an insert: `CG_COST_PDUST` 50 a part, `CG_COST_DBDUST` 73 the drum bus and the master.
- **GATER** is the last entry of `CG_COST_PINS` / `CG_COST_DBINS` (25 and 30; the arrays are FOLD FUZZ PHSR FLNG FRMT
  WIDE CHRS ECHO GATER).
- **A drum sound's two insert slots** (fda4d031): the cost is per voice that sounds, from the slot types that ran
  (`cg_dvoice`): DIST `CG_COST_DVDIST` 96, DYN `CG_COST_DVCOMP` 142, and `CG_COST_DVINS` {97, 140, 92, 106, 134, 182}
  for FILT DUST FOLD FUZZ PHSR FRMT.
- **The master's two insert slots** (`cg_ins_mst`): each running type costs what it costs on the drum bus, DIST and
  FILT at 2 / 5 of the bus's (a stereo pair, not the bus's five channels), and nothing in no slot or at amount 0.
  This is a first model; it was not calibrated in the emulator.
- **JUNO CHORUS** (`INS_JCHORUS`): `CG_COST_JUNO` 140 an instance, about 130 host instructions a sample times 1.04
  (`tests/juno_cho_test.c`); it is not yet a `cpu_costs.py` entry. It counts on a part, on the drum bus and on the
  master (`ifx_w(FXT_JUNO, IFX_MST)`: the master's instance, counted while it still lets go); with the item off its
  checks fold away.
- **JUNO and NOISE voices** are entries 14 and 15 of `CG_VCOST`: 204 and 136 (the JUNO figure is the stage 2 port's
  median, `perf` note: 204 target against 331 for the straight port).
- **VOICES** (per-track voice count): `cg_keep` and the UNISON step ask `trk_nvoice(t)`, the track's own count, not
  the engine's: a part with one voice is never shed, and UNISON is cut to 2 only when the part has more than 2.

## Cost

Measured builds (`measure_costs.py --only CPU_GUARD`, default configuration):
- **Flash:** +1,156 B (optimist ec48329; +1,220 B at ac2f1f2). With ACID built: +2,484 B (at ac2f1f2), because the 303's lite path is compiled in too.
- **RAM:** +64 B.
- **RAMTEXT:** +180 B. The guard's code runs from XIP: it is called FAR once a half. The 180 B come from a few
  bytes in the render (the swarm and UNISON caps) and from the layout of the globals moving with the guard's
  state.
- **CPU:** 0.05 % at 96 MHz. `FM1_HOT` on scenario 10 counted 97,659 instructions in `cg_est`, `cg_pre` and
  `cg_post` over 2 s, of 192 M.

`CPU_GUARD` is off in every profile:
- user-default has 140 B of flash free (ec48329);
- everything-that-fits has 76 B of RAMTEXT free.

## Measurements

Emulator `feat/upstream-merge` (`play_check`, `FM1_CPU_MHZ=96`). Each run is 1 s of boot, then 4 s recorded, on
optimist at `ec48329` (with the SLOOP 2.3 backports).
- **Builds:** user-default without the PIANO set (room for the bench code); scenario 7 adds ACID. The X0X rows use
  the x0x-drums profile with `FELUCCA_BENCH_KIT=38` (the X0X 808).
- **Before** is the same build without the guard. It sheds with SLOOP 2.3's `SHED_FADE` (on by default): two
  halves over 85 % in a row fade out one voice, never the bass or the lead.
- **Scenarios** are in `bench.c`. They are measurement builds only, never shipped. Scenario 10 is new.
- **Counters:**
  - late halves: `felucca_dbg.late`, the DMA moved on before the render ended;
  - halves: the halves rendered;
  - sheds: `shed_count`, or the guard's own count.

| Scenario at 96 MHz | Before: late / sheds | Guard: late / sheds | Guard's highest level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: FM6 ×3, 8 voices + drums | 69 / 15 | 8 / 25 | notes |
| 2: ANALOG SUPER PAD ×3 + drums | 1 / 13 | 1 / 14 | notes (after quality) |
| 3: FM6, ANALOG, FM6 + drums | 15 / 15 | 6 / 27 | notes (after quality) |
| 7: ACID ×3 lines + drums | 98 / 25 | 1 / 0 | notes (nothing to shed: the bass lines stay) |
| 10: FM6 MARK I 8-note cluster + drums | 14 / 18 | 5 / 24 | notes |
| 8: the X0X 808 groove alone | 198 of 595 halves / 0 | 198 of 595 / 0 | notes (nothing to ease) |
| 9: SUPER PAD ×3 + the X0X 808 groove | 397 of 397 halves / 10 | 282 of 511 / 17 | notes |

- **Why fewer late halves:** the notes level fades voices out before a half that the prediction says will run
  late. Shedding alone releases one voice a half, and a long release keeps costing. The guard sheds more notes
  than `SHED_FADE` to get there.
- **Why ACID gets back in time:** the quality level turns off its oversampling. No ACID line loses a note.
  `SHED_FADE` keeps the bass lines too, but nothing else eases them: 98 late halves.
- **The X0X 808 at 96 MHz:** the groove alone runs a third of its halves late. The guard never touches the drums,
  so it cannot help there; with SUPER PAD on top it sheds the pad's voices and the share of late halves falls from
  all to 55 %. The 808 needs a faster clock (or an ease of its own).
  Since perf/x0x-drums (the same samples, cheaper code; guard off) the groove alone has no late half at 96 MHz:
  scenario 8, 966 halves, 0 late, 72 % load (before 717 / 249 late, 102 %); scenario 9 still overloads (454 late
  of 512, 114 %; before 483 of 483, 112 %).
- **Earlier runs** (optimist at `ac2f1f2`, before `SHED_FADE`; the old shed after any half over 85 %), before → guard:
  1: 25/42 → 8/25; 2: 1/23 → 1/14; 3: 20/39 → 6/27; 7: 31/80 → 1/0; 10 (then 8): 21/35 → 5/24.
- **Not measured:** the guard on hardware (XIP cache and flash wait states).

X0X 808's BD / CP and the 909's CH / OH on lanes (mix, `FELUCCA_BENCH_MIX=1`, kit 6); 13 is new: the X0X 808 kit's
groove with the 909's hats on HAT / OPEN HAT (both machines) under scenario 3's parts.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8: X0X 909 groove | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 8: X0X 808 groove | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 8: mix (synthesised 909 + X0X lanes) | 101 of 588 | 8 of 681 | 12 | 8 | 12 |
| 9: SUPER PAD ×3 + X0X 909 | 6 | 6 | 4 | 4 | 254 |
| 9: SUPER PAD ×3 + X0X 808 | 221 of 468 | 148 of 542 | 111 | 101 | 338 |
| 9: SUPER PAD ×3 + mix | 144 of 545 | 76 of 614 | 138 | 66 | 297 |
| 13: FM6, ANALOG, FM6 + both X0X machines | 268 of 421 | 223 of 466 | 136 | 115 | 277 |

- **The model alone changes nothing measurable.** The same builds with the X0X channels in the model but the tails
  as before: 101, 6, 221 and 144 late halves on mots (8 mix, 9 ×3), 12, 4, 111, 138 on x0x-drums. The prediction is
  the last half as measured plus the model's change, so the steady load of the drums was in it already; the model
  only adds their hits, and the early step needs a half over 85 % first.
- **The tails do the work.** In scenario 8 mix, `drums_mix` (the synthesised voices) took 33 % of the core and the
  X0X code about 17 % (`FM1_HOT`). Ending the synthesised voices at -40 dBFS takes the mean load from 80 to 71 %.
  Where the 808 carries the load (9 on kit 38, mots), its tails at -40 dB take it from 96 to 90 %.
- **What is left:** at the top level the guard has shed what it may (never the drums, the bass or the lead): in
  9 on the 808 and in 13 the drums and the kept voices alone are over the half. On mots, DUAL=2 renders parts 2
  and 3 on the second core, so shedding their voices does little for the first core's load (not measured apart).
- **Normal use** (the mots build, panel scenarios, no bench code): the busy song at 96 MHz ran 3 late halves of 858
  before, none after (cpu_q8 154 -> 143); idle, a pad, the drums, the X0X 909 and 808 grooves: none before or after.

The model's cost (mots, guard on): flash +644 B, RAM +16 B, RAMTEXT +80 B (`ds_alive` in `drums_mix` reads the
level). With `CPU_GUARD` off (every profile) the code is the one before.

## Spikes: the reactive path (2026-10-15, feat/overload-monitor)

The user's single-core log while recording as many notes as possible (cpu1-20261015-184640.log, ~4 min): the smoothed load
averaged 63 % (544 samples: 60..69 % for half of them, 91 % the highest), the longest audio interrupt reached 7,693 us (133 % of a
half), and `audio_late` climbed by a steady 12..17 late halves a second (1,567 in all). The guard's level was not logged (the logger
kept only cpu_pct, audio_late, audio_max_us).

What the host shows (`tests/overload_stress.c`, the mots-like flags, instructions a sample counted per half): the render is flat,
the longest half 1.23 x the mean, and a half starting 5 notes or more costs 1.06 x the mean. Chords do not make the spikes; the
FM-1 adds them (other interrupts, flash cache misses, the interrupt held off: not measured here).

Why the guard missed them: it measured the interrupt's own time and stepped after 8 halves in a row over 85 %, or after a late half
that followed one over 85 % (a lone late half was taken for a flash write). Late halves scattered among halves at 60..70 % meet
neither, and a half late because its interrupt began late shows nothing in the interrupt's own time.

The reactive path (`cg_spike`): audio.c tells the guard when the DMA moved on while a half rendered (`cg.late`); each late half adds
64 to a count that loses 1 a half, and at 160 (3 late halves within ~30, or a steady rate of 3 a second and more) the guard reacts,
when the mean load is 50 % or more: one level up; at the top one voice cut and the voices capped one under what sounded (a new
note steals, `CG_VBUDGET` in voice.c), the cap a voice higher after 2 s with no late half. A level a reaction set comes off only
under the load the drop-outs came at less 1/16 of a half (`rcap`), and not while the voices are capped. Without a late half nothing
changes: the goldens are the same (regress with the guard built: 0 changed).

The stress (60 s, the clock set for a 66 % mean; the FM-1's spikes stood in for by a model):

| spikes | no guard | the guard before | with the reactive path |
|---|---|---|---|
| none (the host's costs) | 0 late | 0 | 0 |
| 12 % of the halves + 50 % of the mean | 4.45 % | 0.08 % | 0.08 % |
| every 11th half + 55 % (isolated) | 6.39 % | 0.82 % | 0.60 % |
| every 11th interrupt held off 40 % of a half | 8.24 % | 8.22 % (never moves) | 0.97 % (mean 54 %) |

The last row is the log's signature (a steady rate of late halves at a 66 % mean, the guard idle).
